# WV cross-model triage

**2026-08-24.** West Virginia was one of two states (with NJ) that had never been
triaged — the harness had run it, nobody had read the residual. One cause
accounts for 30–51% of its clean misses, and it is TAXSIM's.

## The finding

`v37_state_property_tax_credit` is **1000 on 100% of the miss cluster** and
nonzero on **0.2%** of matching records. The mechanism is arithmetic, not
statistical.

West Virginia's Homestead Excess Property Tax Credit pays property tax *in excess
of 4% of household income*, capped at **$1,000**. TAXSIM computes the excess term
without flooring the income side, so where its household income `v30` is
**negative** the term is positive from a **zero property-tax base**:

```
excess = proptax − 0.04 × v30
       = 0      − 0.04 × (−452,759)
       = +18,110   →  capped at 1,000
```

The cluster's median `v30` is **−$452,759**, driven by `other_inc` at a median
of **−$587,199**. 62–70% of firing records have `v30 < 0`, and 82–88% sit exactly
at the $1,000 cap. `v31_state_rent_expense` is zero for every one of them.

**So TAXSIM pays the maximum property-tax credit to records with no property
tax, purely because income is negative.** This is the same failure mode as the
MI home-heating credit at `v30 == 1.01` (issues doc T6): a means test that goes
degenerate at pathological income.

## Effect

| year | clean n | base | excluding `v37 > 0` | gain |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2017 | 6,605 | 0.9131 | **0.9686** | +5.6pp |
| 2018 | 8,136 | 0.9024 | **0.9547** | +5.2pp |
| 2019 | 8,197 | 0.9040 | **0.9568** | +5.3pp |
| 2020 | 7,818 | 0.8686 | 0.9186 | +5.0pp |

**Three of four years clear the 0.95 bar.** 2020 remains short and is not
attributed.

## The control

The predicate must fire on the mechanism, not on record complexity — the
discipline the 2026-08-22 class sweep established. It does: **`v37` is exactly
zero in 34 of 51 states**, and nonzero only where a property-tax credit exists
(IL 1,070 records, WV 470, MN 461, CT 406, NY 315, VT 288, MI 273, DC 238 in
2019). A predicate that tracked complexity would fire everywhere.

## Why exclude rather than annotate

Two sub-classes, both justifying exclusion for different reasons:

- **`v30 < 0` (62–70%)** — a TAXSIM defect. Worth filing upstream alongside T6,
  since it is the second instance of the same degenerate-means-test pattern.
- **`v30 ≥ 0` with real property tax** — genuine claimants, and **Tier-1
  blocked** for us: neither property tax nor rent exists in the PUF for
  non-itemizers. This is the class `state_weights/plan.md` §J3 already
  enumerates (RI-1040H, MT's rebate, VT and MN renter credits, WI's homestead
  credit, the IL/CT property-tax credit inputs). **WV belongs on that list and
  was missing only because it had never been triaged.**

Both exceed the $100 tolerance, so neither is a sub-$100 annotate.

One row added to `known_differences.csv`, predicate
`v37_state_property_tax_credit > 0`.

## What remains

- **2020 at 0.9186** — unattributed. The largest remaining modes are ±$1,000
  residue (13 records), −$520 (7) and diffuse sub-$250 differences.
- **The PolicyEngine window was not examined** (0.923–0.939, all four years
  short of the bar by 1–3pp). Worth checking whether PE nets the same credit —
  the RI lesson was that a Tier-1-blocked class excluded on one leg needs the
  twin row on the other whenever the second model also implements the credit.
- **NJ is still untriaged**, and is much further from the bar (TAXSIM
  0.71–0.86 clean, PE 2021 0.74).
