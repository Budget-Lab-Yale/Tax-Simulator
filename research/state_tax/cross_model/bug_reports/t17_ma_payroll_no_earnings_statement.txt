TAXSIM-35, Massachusetts, TY2018, single, age 70, taxable pension
40,000, gross Social Security 20,000, NO wages and no
self-employment income.

Expected: siitax = 1,779.90. This filer paid no FICA, Medicare or
Railroad Retirement contributions during the year and made no
contribution to a U.S. or Massachusetts public retirement system,
so the Form 1 line 11 deduction is zero. Massachusetts excludes
Social Security from its base entirely, leaving 40,000 less the
4,400 personal exemption and the 700 age-65 exemption = 34,900, at
5.1% = 1,779.90.

TAXSIM returns siitax = 1,677.90 -- an implied line 11 deduction of
exactly 2,000 on a return with no earnings at all.

The trigger is positive gross Social Security. It is a FLAT 2,000,
not a computed contribution: holding the return otherwise identical
and varying gssi alone, TY2018 Massachusetts,
  gssi      0 -> implied deduction     0 (correct)
  gssi  5,000 -> implied deduction 2,000  (7.65% of gssi = 383)
  gssi 10,000 -> implied deduction 2,000  (7.65% of gssi = 765)
  gssi 20,000 -> implied deduction 2,000  (7.65% of gssi = 1,530)
  gssi 30,000 -> implied deduction 2,000
  gssi 60,000 -> implied deduction 2,000
so it is not FICA computed on the Social Security amount either --
5,000 of Social Security draws the full 2,000.

It is ADDITIVE on top of a legitimate wage-based deduction, so it
also hits working filers, not only retirees:
  age 70, wages 40,000, no gssi     -> implied 2,000 (correct)
  age 70, wages 40,000, gssi 20,000 -> implied 4,000 (-102.00)
  age 70, pension 40,000, no gssi   -> implied     0 (correct)
  age 70, pension 40,000, gssi 20k  -> implied 2,000 (-102.00)
  age 40, pension 40,000, no gssi   -> implied     0 (correct)
Age is irrelevant; the presence of gssi is what produces it.

The likely source is Medicare premiums withheld from Social
Security, which Massachusetts expressly disallows: the 2018 booklet
(p.10) states that Medicare PREMIUMS withheld from Social Security
or retirement payments are not deductible, in contrast to the
Medicare tax withheld from wages, which is. If that is the source,
the amount should in any case be the premiums actually withheld
rather than the statutory cap.

This is the same shape as the Utah retirement-credit issue reported
separately: a flat state amount keyed to positive gssi alone.

On our TY2018 validation sample the ours-high side of this affects
496 records, 89.1% of them Social Security recipients and 71.8%
aged 65 or over, median age 69, each overstated by about 102
dollars of deduction value.
