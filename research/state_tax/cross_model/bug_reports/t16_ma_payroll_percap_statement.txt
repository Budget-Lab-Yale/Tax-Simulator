TAXSIM-35, Massachusetts, TY2018, married filing jointly, both
spouses age 40, wages 60,000 each (120,000 total), no dependents.

Expected: siitax = 5,467.20. Each spouse pays 4,590 of FICA
(7.65% x 60,000), so each reaches the separate $2,000 cap on the
Form 1 line 11 deduction, giving 4,000. Massachusetts AGI 120,000
less 4,000 of deductions less the 8,800 joint personal exemption
is 107,200, at 5.1% = 5,467.20.

TAXSIM returns siitax = 5,569.20, which is 107,200 + 2,000 taxed at
5.1% -- an implied deduction of 2,000, i.e. the cap applied once per
return. The 2018 Form 1 has SEPARATE lines 11a and 11b and the
booklet (p.10) reads: 'Enter in lines 11a and 11b the amount you,
and your spouse if filing jointly, paid to Social Security (FICA),
Medicare or Railroad Retirement ... you may deduct those
contributions, up to a maximum of $2,000.' Each line carries its own
'Not more than $2,000' instruction, so the cap is per person and
non-transferable between spouses.

Probed across four joint shapes, all TY2018 Massachusetts, showing
the implied deduction pinned at 2,000 regardless of the second
earner's own contributions:
  60,000 / 0      -> implied 2,000 (correct, one earner)
  60,000 / 60,000 -> implied 2,000, expected 4,000  (+102.00)
  60,000 / 30,000 -> implied 2,000, expected 4,000  (+102.00)
  60,000 / 10,000 -> implied 2,000, expected 2,765  ( +39.02)
A single filer at 60,000 returns the correct 2,000, so the single
case is unaffected.

On our TY2018 validation sample this affects 741 records, 96.4% of
them joint returns with median AGI 136,874, each understated by
about 102 dollars of deduction value.
