Budget Lab · Tax-Simulator · design decision memo

Taxing capital gains on accrual

Two things to sign off on: how we approximate mark-to-market from the data we have, and what we do about assets you can't realistically mark — the deferral-charge question, which is a policy call, not a plumbing detail.

2026-07-11 branch wealth companion: other/accrual_tax/DESIGN.md status: pre-build
01

Where we landed

An accrual tax has three parts. The first two are settled; the third is the decision this memo exists for.

A · Ongoing flow — tax the accrual columns directly

Settled

Tax the imputed accrual (accruals.* — each asset class's historical-average total return × current value) each year, and switch off realization taxation on those assets. Because realizations are no longer taxed, we never need to track basis or follow a taxpayer over time — it's stateless. This is a legitimate proxy for the go-forward flow of a mark-to-market tax.

B · Transition — the built-in gain at enactment

Settled

The flow only captures appreciation after enactment. Gains that accrued before it (the stock value − basis on day one) are a separate one-time base, phased in over several years — as in the real Wyden/Biden proposals. Sizable, and computed from the balance sheet, not from accruals.*.

C · Illiquid assets — MTM or a deferral charge?

Decide

Private businesses and homes can't realistically be marked to market every year (no cash for tax on paper gains; annual valuation is hard). The alternative is to keep taxing them at sale/death but add an interest charge for the deferral. This is a genuine policy fork with revenue, distribution, and complexity consequences — section 04.

02

Why the data forces this shape

Tax-Data is a repeated representative cross-section, not a panel — the same household can't be followed year to year. That single fact rules some designs in and others out.

What works

  • Taxing accruals.* — it's a within-year imputed flow, so no differencing across years is needed.
  • A deferral charge — it lands on the gain stock at a within-year event (a sale or death), which the cross-section represents cleanly.
  • Deemed realization at death — already built, and consistent for exactly this reason.

What doesn't

  • Literal per-record mark-to-market — value(t) − value(t−1) needs a panel we don't have.
  • But we don't need it: accruals.* is the flow proxy, and taxing it sidesteps the missing panel entirely.
03

What the flow proxy captures — and misses

Being honest about the accruals.* approach, so the trade-offs are on the table before we commit.

Captures

  • Go-forward appreciation, by asset class.
  • Concentration across the wealth distribution — the top holds far more equities, so it still bears most of the tax.
  • Stateless: no basis ledger, no cohort machinery, no new SLURM phase.

Misses

  • Year-to-year volatility and down-year losses — the rate is a smooth average, always positive.
  • Return heterogeneity within an asset class — everyone gets the class-average rate.
  • The pre-enactment built-in gain — handled separately by the transition calc (part B), not by the flow.
04

The deferral charge — the policy decision

For assets we don't mark annually, the question is how hard to lean against deferral. A plain realization tax lets gains compound untaxed — an interest-free loan from the Treasury. A deferral (retrospective) charge claws that back.

The decision

For private businesses and homes: pure MTM, a deferral charge, or exemption (homes already have the §121 exclusion)? The leading hybrid is MTM the marketable assets, deferral-charge the illiquid ones — but that's a call for you, not a default.

Mark-to-market

Tax accrued gains every year (via the flow proxy). Cleanest economics, no lock-in.

Cost: asks illiquid owners to pay cash on paper gains; leans on annual valuations the model imputes rather than observes.

Deferral charge

Tax at sale/death as today, plus interest for the years the gain went untaxed. Preserves liquidity; approximates MTM's present value.

Cost: needs a holding period, which the data doesn't carry.

Making the deferral charge computable

The holding period isn't in the data — but we can impute it from the very columns we couldn't tax directly. The gain-to-basis ratio and the accrual rate imply how long an asset has been held:

r // implied return = accrualsk / valuek
H // implied holding period = ln(valuek / basisk) / ln(1 + r)
charge = τ · gaink · [ (1 + i)H − 1 ]  // i = statutory deferral rate

The charge rides the existing realization and death machinery, so it stays stateless and cheap. Two conventions to pin down: the interest formula (simple compounding vs. an annuitized lookback) and the rate i.

Open decisions for sign-off

Primary

Illiquid-asset treatment

MTM everything, or MTM liquid + deferral-charge illiquid, or exempt the primary home under a cap.

Formula

Deferral-charge convention & rate

Simple compounding (1+i)^H − 1 vs. annuitized lookback; statutory rate i.

Transition

Built-in gain phase-in

One-time recognition at enactment vs. an N-year schedule.

Rate

Rate on the accrual base

Ordinary rates vs. a preferential mark-to-market rate; whether it varies over time.

Scope

Applicability threshold

Wyden-style net-worth / income floor (e.g. >$1B or >$100M for 3 yrs). Both fields exist in the data.

Required

Turn off the lock-in response on marked assets

Under MTM there's no lock-in, so kg_dynamics must not also model a realization response on marked classes. The per-class regime already supports this — a consistency edit, not a choice.

07

Build cost

Under this approach the whole thing is a stateless calculator, in the same category as the corporate-incidence channel — no new cross-year state, no new SLURM phase.

  • New reform-overridable accrual.yaml (dormant at baseline): per-class treatment, rate, deferral rate, phase-in, threshold.
  • New calc_accrual() in src/calc/functions/tax/, called from do_taxes; mirrors calc_wealth.
  • Flow, transition, and deferral charge are all within-year and stateless.
  • Only a literal cohort mark-to-market bathtub would be a large build — and this approach deliberately avoids needing it.
