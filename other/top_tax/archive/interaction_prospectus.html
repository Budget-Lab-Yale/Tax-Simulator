Part I

The substantive vision

A microsimulation in which the top-end tax bases are physically connected on record-level balance sheets, and the behavioral elasticities are equilibrium objects that move with the policy package.

1.1  Why not an ETI calculation

The standard approach prices each instrument with a fixed, policy-invariant elasticity and adds the results. That fails at the top of the distribution for two reasons this model fixes. First, the bases overlap: the same dollar of wealth is capital income during life, an estate at death, and wealth-tax base in between — so hitting it anywhere shrinks the other bases. We track that on actual record-level balance sheets rather than through an assumed cross-elasticity. Second, the elasticities are policy-dependent: the realization elasticity is an equilibrium object that collapses when step-up ends; the entity-shifting elasticity depends on the corporate–individual differential; the income-conversion response depends on the whole tax vector; the financing response depends on the total bill. The Lucas critique bites hardest exactly where this report lives — large reforms far outside the variation that identified the reduced-form estimates.

The empirical backing for treating single-base elasticities as misspecified is direct: with valid (Weber) instruments, Mortenson (2016) finds the own-tax ordinary-income ETI around the Bush cuts is indistinguishable from zero until the capital-gains cross-rate enters the regression — and once it does, his cleanest specification delivers own and cross responses that are almost exactly equal and opposite (+0.79 / −0.77), the signature of a response driven by the differential between bases rather than by any single rate.

1.2  What the model already closes

Four cross-base loops are live — three mechanical, one behavioral:

  1. Wealth ↔ capital income ↔ estate (the bathtub). A share s of any during-life tax increase (income + payroll + wealth) is financed out of wealth rather than consumption (s calibrated 2026-07-07: 0.1 → 0.8 across the wealth distribution). The erosion compounds, shrinks future dividends/interest/gains/pass-through flows, and drains into the estate and wealth-tax bases. One channel, three interactions.
  2. Corporate → individual. A corporate hike cuts payout flows, marks down exposed equity (price margin on gains, basis fixed), scales payouts (quantity margin), and the individual-side offset materializes endogenously in conventional receipts. Feeds the bathtub as external income (corp_dY_exog).
  3. Death regime → realization elasticity. In kg_dynamics, realization solves a Bellman problem whose continuation value depends on the death regime (step-up / carryover / deemed, per asset class). Ending step-up mechanically lowers the realization elasticity — a naive calculation must assume this; we derive it.
  4. Entity shifting (behavioral). Pearce–Prisinzano semi-elasticity (≈0.63) moves business income across the corporate / pass-through boundary as the differential moves.

Also on hand: evasion module (built 2026-07-07, §1.3), charity elasticities, deemed-death avoidance haircut, NIIT and QBI levers, estate and wealth-tax law fully parameterized.

1.4  What σ actually measures

σ is the income-classification margin available to people who control the form of their own pay. Concretely, ranked by pool size:

  1. Owner-manager compensation-setting at closely held firms — the dominant pool. The owner chooses annually how much to take as salary/bonus (ordinary) versus leave in the firm and extract as appreciation — LTCG at sale, or untaxed under step-up. Smith–Yagan–Zidar–Zwick (QJE 2019) estimate ~75% of top pass-through profits are returns to owner human capital: at the top, the labor/capital boundary is already largely an accounting convention. That finding defines σ’s pool.
    Legal-channel precision (2026-07-08). “Leave in the firm” works as stated only for C corporations — pass-throughs cannot retain taxable earnings (K-1 income is taxed currently whether or not distributed). For pass-through owners the real channel is sweat equity: underpaying your own labor while it builds enterprise value realized as gain on eventual sale — available only to the extent the business is appreciable. The model’s destination (the household unrealized-gain stock) is agnostic about the wrapper, and since Δconv = σ·ΔW·pool makes pool breadth and σ one economic knob, the pool’s over-breadth on pass-through legs is absorbed by the σ = 0.08 calibration. Fuller account: sigma_explainer.md.
  2. Founder-equity structuring — the new-formation flow. Sub-market founder salary while effort accrues as stock: 83(b) elections on near-zero-basis shares, QSBS (§1202 — up to $10M tax-free, so this route’s effective rate is ~0), ISO-vs-RSU mix at private companies.
  3. Service partners: carried interest and profits interests (Rev. Proc. 93-27) — PE, hedge funds, real estate, any partnership can pay for labor in capital-gain form. Mostly inframarginal to small wedge changes; the elastic piece is new structuring volume.

What σ deliberately is not: RSU and option wages at public companies (ordinary at vest/exercise — those escape routes were legislated shut; the modern top W-2 base is the selection residue of past escape), business income crossing the entity boundary (the P–P module), realization timing (the Bellman), underreporting (the evasion module), and working less (excluded). The historical record shows the margin moves when the wedge is large — under the 91% top rates executive pay migrated wholesale into qualified stock options and deferred compensation, and Goolsbee (2000) found the apparent response to the 1993 hike was mostly option-exercise behavior at exactly this boundary.

1.5  Functional form

The forcing variable is the conversion wedge — the gap between the top ordinary rate and the effective rate actually paid on the equity path:

τ_eq(t)  =  τ_cg(t) × [ ρ·δ_defer  +  (1−ρ)·x_regime ]        (crude accrual-equivalent)

W(t)     =  τ_ord_top(t)  −  τ_eq(t)                          (conversion wedge)
ρ = share of accrued gains realized during life; δ_defer = PV discount for deferral; x_regime = 0 under step-up, ≈δ_death under deemed-at-death, below δ_defer under carryover. Illustrative: current law ≈ 41% − 9% ≈ 32pp wedge; gains-at-ordinary + deemed-at-death collapses it to single digits. Exposition only as of 2026-07-08 — this closed form is dead as code; the production τeq is extracted from the kg machinery itself (§1.5a).

1.5a  How τeq actually works — in plain terms

The question τeq answers. Take one dollar of top compensation. Paid as salary, its tax life is trivial: taxed once, this year, at the ordinary marginal rate — roughly 41 cents, done. Now suppose the owner instead leaves that dollar in the firm, where it becomes a dollar of unrealized capital gain. What tax does that dollar pay? There is no single answer — it depends on everything that happens to it over the rest of the owner’s life. τeq is the average over all of those futures, expressed in present-value cents per dollar: “a dollar entering the gain state today, held by someone of age a in year t, will eventually pay τeq(a,t) cents of tax, in today’s money.” The conversion wedge is then just the two tax prices side by side: W = 41¢ − τeq.

The life of a converted dollar. Once in the gain state, the dollar faces exactly the fates the model already tracks for every other unrealized gain:

Realized while alive A slice of the stock is sold each year — at the cell’s own realization rate (the fixed, planned, and Bellman-decision buckets combined) — and taxed at that year’s capital-gains rate.
Held to death under step-up Basis resets; the deferred tax vanishes. Contribution to τeq: zero. (This is why τeq falls with age under current law — an 85-year-old’s dollar is very likely to escape entirely.)
Held to death under carryover Heirs inherit the basis; the tax arrives later, when they sell — the same discounted death burden the Bellman already prices.
Held to death under deemed realization Taxed at death, nearly in full. Under this regime τeq rises with age — less runway to defer.
Bequeathed to charity Never taxed, under any regime.
Every future payment Discounted back to today at β.

The arithmetic is bookkeeping, not optimization. Nothing new is being solved. The kg machinery has already decided how fast each (age, year) cell realizes gains, how likely each cell is to die, and what death costs under each regime. τeq simply follows one dollar through those existing rules and adds up the discounted taxes. Formally it is a backward recursion — the expected tax on a dollar held at age a equals this year’s expected tax plus the discounted expected tax on whatever survives to age a+1:

τ_eq(a,t)  =  [share realized this year] · τ(a,t)
           +  [share dying this year]   · death_burden(a,t) · τ(a,t)
           +  [share surviving, unrealized] · β · τ_eq(a+1, t+1)
Every input is a number the model already produces for the bathtub: the realization share is the scenario rate r_S (fixed + retimed-planned + Bellman-decision buckets), the death burden is the regime-mix object c_phi_eff (0 under step-up, θ-weighted under carryover, ≈full under deemed, net of the charity share), mortality and β are the Bellman’s own. Computed twice per scenario-year — under baseline policy (τ_eq_B) and under the reform (τ_eq_S).

Why it is verified by finite difference (ruled 2026-07-08). Two details make an informal formula easy to get subtly wrong, and review caught both: (a) the stock σ’s dollars join realizes at the scenario rate r_S including the re-timed planned bucket, not the Bellman’s internal baseline-scaled version; (b) in the recurrence, people who die during year t die holding their full stock — they do not realize first. So the build order is: first compute τeq the unarguable way — literally inject a test dollar into each (age, year) cell of the actual recurrence code, run it forward, and measure the present value of the extra tax collected. Then implement the fast recursion above and require it to equal that finite-difference answer cell-by-cell in automated tests. The recursion is what runs in production; the finite difference is the proof it is right. The test dollar is injected at end-of-year, exactly as the σ inflow is, so the wedge prices precisely the life a converted dollar will actually live.

What it looks like in numbers. A mid-50s owner under current law: realization runs a few percent a year, much of the stock rides to death, and death usually means step-up — so τeq lands near 9% against a ~41% top ordinary rate: a ~32pp wedge. The same owner under gains-at-ordinary plus deemed-at-death: every branch of the tree now ends in tax at a high rate and deferral buys almost nothing, so τeq climbs toward the ordinary rate and the wedge collapses to single digits. That collapse — τeq as a function of the package — is the entire mechanism: it is why the model’s ordinary-rate ETI endogenously shrinks when a package closes the capital-side exits.

Measured (2026-07-08, full-sample runs). On the real grid under current law, τeq runs 5.6–11.8% across ages (pool-weighted τeq/τcg ≈ 0.395), peaking in the late 40s and falling toward death exactly as the step-up logic predicts — the mid-50s illustration above is what the machinery actually produces. The in-run finite-difference check passed cell-by-cell for all years of every run. One measured refinement to the sizing intuition: a statutory CG cut moves the pool-weighted wedge by roughly half its naive τeq/τ scaling, because the cell-aggregate CG rate moves less than the top statutory rate and the Bellman partially offsets (realize faster at the cheaper rate). And because τeq is a forward-looking PV, announced future rate changes move conversions a year before they take effect — anticipation is built in by construction.

The response is a semi-elasticity on the gated pool, driven by each record’s own wedge change:

W_i(t)      =  mtr_ordinary_i(t)  −  τ_eq(age_i, t)          (per-record wedge)

Δconv_i(t)  =  σ · ( W_i(t) − W_i₀(t) ) · pool_i

pool_i  =  wages_i  +  0.75 · active_passthrough_i        (gated owner-managers)

wages′      =  wages − Δconv          ← leaves the ordinary base
gain state′ =  gain state + Δconv      ← still in the system
σ in units of % of the pool per percentage point of wedge change. The ordinary leg is personal — the record’s own calculator-measured MTRs, per earner and per income leg; the equity leg is the cell-level τ_eq keyed by age. No phase-in (revised 2026-07-08): a memoryless annual response, the same convention as entity shifting and evasion. Converted dollars are taxed at realization, at death (deemed), by heirs (carryover), or never (step-up) — per the package. That is the mechanism, not a leak.

The key structural property: σ is a fixed parameter, but the reduced-form ETI it generates is policy-dependent. Under current law (a ~32pp wedge, step-up open) it reproduces literature-scale ETIs. Under a package taxing gains at ordinary rates with deemed realization at death, W barely moves when the top rate rises, so the conversion response ≈ 0 and the ordinary-rate ETI endogenously collapses. This operationalizes Kopczuk (2005): the elasticity is a function of base breadth.

1.6  The full layered system

One gated owner-manager record, one year. Baseline legs: w (wages), b (pass-through profit), U (stock of unrealized gains). A package sets (τo, τcg, death regime, τcorp, τestate, τwealth). The layers fire in a pinned order; each consumes the previous layer’s output, so no dollar can move twice.

LAYER 1 — σ, conversion (conservation)         forcing: the wedge W
  Δconv  =  σ · (W_i(t) − W_i₀) · pool_i
  w′  =  w − Δconv_w        b′  =  b − Δconv_b        U′  =  U + Δconv

LAYER 2 — entity shifting (conservation)        forcing: τ_pt − τ_corp differential
  Δshift  =  0.63 · Δdifferential · b′
  b″  =  b′ − Δshift           corp_base  +=  Δshift      (taxed at τ_corp)

LAYER 3 — evasion (leak)                        forcing: own net-of-tax rate
  b‴  =  b″ · (1 + e_pt · Δln(1−τ_o))       e_wages = 0 (third-party reported)

LAYER 4 — realization (timing, not existence)   forcing: CG rate + death regime
  g(t)  =  realization_policy( U′, τ_cg, regime )        (kg Bellman)
  remainder rides to next year, or to death: deemed / carryover / step-up

LAYER 5 — taxes computed, then the bathtub (balance sheet, not income)
  ΔT⁰  =  Δ(liab_iit + pr − deemed + wealth)  vs baseline
  F    =  ΔT⁰ − ΔY_exog                       (corp shock netted out)
  P(t+1)  =  G(t)·P(t) + s·F    →    value.* scaled down
  → future dividends / interest / gains, estate base, wealth base all shrink — once
The corporate channel (when the package moves τcorp) applies at the head of the pass as an external income shock ΔY_exog — netted out of the bathtub forcing F so income that fell externally is not also “financed.”

One dollar of would-be top salary, all branches:

Stays as wages Taxed at τo now.
Converted by σ Taxed at PV τeq: the CG rate at realization, or the deemed rate at death, or zero under step-up — the package decides.
Shifted entity form (profit-leg dollars) Taxed at τcorp now, in the corporate base.
Evaded (Schedule C / pass-through / rent legs only — never wages) Zero, forever: the one true leak.
Whichever tax is paid A share s is financed from wealth → future capital-income, estate, and wealth-tax bases shrink — exactly once.

Two distinct double-counting risks, resolved differently:

Mechanical double-counting is impossible by construction. Modules apply sequentially (σ → entity shifting → evasion), each on the frame the previous one produced. A dollar σ converts is gone before P–P computes its flow. The build carries an assert on the ordering plus a per-record diagnostic of all three flows so overlap is auditable, not assumed away.

Calibration double-counting is real — and residualization is the fix. σ’s pool includes the labor-content slice of pass-through profits, which is also entity shifting’s and evasion’s territory; and the literature estimates for each margin were made in data where the other margins were operating (P–P’s 0.63 contains labor-content conversion; Mortenson’s −0.77 contains everything). Calibrating σ stand-alone from those numbers and letting it share the leg would overstate the summed response. So σ is calibrated residually: it is whatever remains of the target total ETI after a perturbation run with evasion, entity shifting, and realization already switched on — see the revision below. The literature numbers are reinterpreted as brackets on the total cross-base response — which is what the validation exhibit tests, and the more meaningful economic object anyway.

Revised 2026-07-08. Residual calibration is dropped: σ is asserted at 0.2 / 0.6 / 0.9, and the calibration double-count named above is accepted and monitored rather than eliminated by construction. The monitor is the perturbation run: the full stack’s 2×2 own/cross elasticity matrix is compared informally against the literature brackets at build time, and σ or the pool is revisited if the stack over-responds — an iteration loop, not a formal gate. Outcome (same day): the monitor fired exactly as designed. The stack over-responded at σ = 0.6 (ETI 0.431), the leave-one-out leg measured the non-σ stack at 0.223, and σ landed at 0.08 — so the design converged back to residual-style discipline after all, just executed as one measured iteration rather than a pre-registered procedure. The literature anchors survive as brackets on the total response, which is what they always identified.

The honest residual soft spot: with a shared pool, the split of a package’s response between “goes to the corporate base” (P–P) and “goes to the gain state” (σ) is set by two parameters the evidence cannot separately identify. The split matters — it determines revenue composition (current corporate receipts vs deferred capital gains) and the strength of the death-regime interaction. It is therefore an explicit, named, sweepable parameter, not a hidden assumption. Revised 2026-07-08 The split parameter is dropped: the composition of the response is a reported output of the per-record flow diagnostics, not an assumed dial — sequential module order alone prevents double-moves.

One consistency check worth recording: σ and the bathtub compose correctly without coordination. When σ fires, wages fall, the current-year tax bill falls, and the bathtub sees less tax to finance — correct, because the converted dollar was saved (parked in the firm). When it is realized years later, the tax appears and the bathtub responds then.

The symmetry invariant is stated in wedge units. The model’s response to +Δτord must mirror an equal-and-opposite wedge change — not an equal statutory CG change, because dτeq/dτcg < 1 (the deferral discount). Mortenson’s equal-and-opposite finding cannot distinguish these; the statutory asymmetry is therefore a prediction the model makes that the reduced form cannot — a fourth structural finding, free.

Part II

The presentation vision

The interaction matrix is the centerpiece, delivered as an interactive explorer over an exactly-computed policy grid, with the greenbook standalones and a small set of framing exhibits around it.

2.1  Why the matrix headlines, and not a revenue-max number

An unconstrained Laffer search returns “max out every lever except the CG rate” — fragile, parameter-sensitive, and boring, and it invites fights about the argmax rather than the mechanism. The interaction structure is the part no other model can produce: a corporate hike lowers the yield of a CG hike by X%; ending step-up raises it by Y%; a wealth tax cannibalizes Z% of the estate score. Every such sentence is robust across the elasticity band even when levels shift. The revenue-max search (and the quadratic-surrogate machinery specced for it) is deferred entirely — the grid below would power it later at near-zero marginal cost if revisited.

2.2  The interactive explorer

A self-contained HTML/JS exhibit: one dropdown per lever, and for the selected package a readout of —

  • Revenue, 10-year window and 30-year window, as two separate figures (never averaged), at the central elasticity bundle each at the low / central / high elasticity bundle (bands dropped 2026-07-08; reinstatable later as additional runs);
  • Interaction decomposition: package total vs the sum of its standalone pieces, in dollars and percent — the matrix cell, generalized to the whole grid;
  • Leakage share = 1 − conventional/static — the “how much escapes” number, already a model output;
  • Wealth erosion: cumulative top-1% private-wealth decumulation from the bathtub state — how much of each revenue dollar is financed by dissaving rather than consumption. No elasticity calculation can produce this axis;
  • Escape-route panel: the conversion wedge W under the selected package, next to current law (§2.4).

Every displayed number is an exact pipeline run — no interpolation asterisks. A simplified static version (a small multiple of the central pairwise matrix at central intensities) goes in the report body for readers who never open the explorer.

2.4  Framing exhibits

  • The escape-route exhibit. One bar per package: the conversion wedge W. Current law ≈ 32pp; gains-at-ordinary + deemed ≈ single digits. This chart is the mechanism behind the headline finding — it shows in one number why identical top-rate hikes score differently across packages, and why the ETI is not a constant.
  • Window vs steady state. The 10-year and 30-year results diverge because bathtub erosion compounds; the divergence is reported as a named finding (“wealth taxes look better in the window than in the steady state”).
  • The validation matrix (§1.8): the model’s 2×2 own/cross elasticities against Mortenson/Dowd brackets — the micro-scale mirror of the policy-scale interaction matrix.
  • Distribution discipline. Per-package distribution tables (static, per convention) confirm burden below P99 ≈ 0 — the “greenbook for the rich” check, reported as a diagnostic rather than imposed as a constraint.

2.5  Conventions, fixed up front

  1. The objective is total package revenue against a common baseline, never stacked attribution — with the endogenous corporate offset, stacked rows are not order-invariant. Attribution order, where shown, is a labeled presentation convention.
  2. Two horizons, two outputs. 10-year window and 30-year window reported separately; no blending.
  3. The matrix is a fan, not a line. Every cell is conditional on (ψ, wealth-avoidance elasticities, σ, s, corporate corners). Tier 2 publishes the band over the low/central/high bundle. Band runs are deferred (2026-07-08); the conditionality is stated in the report rather than published as a band, for now.
  4. Out of scope, stated in the report: mark-to-market and trusts (deferred entirely; on-model instruments only), migration/expatriation (off-model haircut band on wealth-tax results), and the revenue-max search (deferred; the grid can power it later).
