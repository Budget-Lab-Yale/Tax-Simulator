Step-up caps the capital-gains rate as a revenue tool; reforming it raises the ceiling

Ten-year conventional revenue (FY2027–36), $B · behavioral responses included · shared scale

Deemed realization at death Carryover basis Current law — step-up in basis

Under step-up, the capital-gains rate is nearly spent by the low 40s. Revenue rises to about $564B and then flattens — each five points past 40% adds under $10B, because higher rates just push more gains to be held until death and escape tax entirely. Reforming step-up changes the picture two ways. It raises the starting level: at today's 20% rate, taxing gains at death already collects ~$706B (carryover ~$252B) with no rate change at all. And it keeps the rate productive: with deferral closed, the same rate increases keep pulling in revenue, reaching ~$1.86T at 50% — roughly 3.3× the step-up ceiling at a 40% rate. Carryover sits between the two. Source: Tax-Simulator dials-v2 (current physics, η=2.4825); step-up curve is direct solo-rate runs, the carryover and deemed curves are surrogate-composed and anchored to direct package runs at the 20% and 25% points (deemed 20%→$706B, 25%→$1,106B match exactly). Curves are total conventional revenue, so they include the death-regime's own yield; the shape, not any single dollar, is the point.
