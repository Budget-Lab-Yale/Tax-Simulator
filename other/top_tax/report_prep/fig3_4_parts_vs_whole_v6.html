Figures 3 and 4

The same corporate and capital-gains rate increases, added one policy at a time — first under current law, then on a base that also taxes gains at death. Bars show each policy's static and collected revenue as a share of GDP, colored by the tax base where the revenue is collected. Shown for the third decade, once the long-run realization response has largely played out.

Figure 3

Revenue from a corporate and capital-gains rate increase, static, after spillovers, and collected, 2047–56

Third decade (FY2047–56), % of GDP · slim bar = static estimate, middle bar = after cross-base spillovers, full bar = collected · ▲ / ▼ = collected minus static

Capital gains Corporate Ordinary income Estate slim bar = static estimate middle bar = after cross-base spillovers

Each policy is shown three times. The slim bar is the static estimate, the first-order tax increase with every base held fixed. The middle bar adds the cross-base spillovers that follow mechanically from the policy — corporate income repricing equity and pass-through earnings, and tax paid out of wealth draining the estate base — with taxpayer behavior still held fixed. The full bar adds the behavioral response, and is the revenue actually collected. In the third decade the capital-gains rate increase has a static estimate of 0.14% of GDP, 0.12% after spillovers and 0.06% collected: most of the gap is deferral of realizations under step-up in basis. The corporate rate increase asks 0.24% of GDP, holds 0.21% after spillovers and collects 0.18%, as C-corporate income is recharacterized as pass-through, draining corporate revenue and lifting ordinary-income revenue. The package collects 64 cents of every static dollar. Both policies also reduce estate revenue slightly (shown left of zero). A payroll head of at most 0.002% of GDP is omitted. Source: The Budget Lab Tax-Simulator, dials-v6 surrogate.

Figure 4

Revenue from the same increases with gains taxed at death, static, after spillovers, and collected, 2047–56

Third decade (FY2047–56), % of GDP · slim bar = static estimate, middle bar = after cross-base spillovers, full bar = collected · ▲ / ▼ = collected minus static · shared scale with Figure 3

Capital gains Corporate Ordinary income Estate slim bar = static estimate middle bar = after cross-base spillovers

When gains are taxed at death, the same capital-gains rate increase collects 0.11% of GDP against 0.06% under step-up, and the package as a whole collects 95 cents of every static dollar — where the rate-only version in Figure 3 collects 64. Taxing gains at death is the one row where the middle bar sits on the slim one: it moves no other base mechanically, and its whole difference from the static estimate is behavioral, since closing the deferral raises realizations during life as well as at death. Revenue from gains taxed at death is folded into the capital-gains base. The corporate increase behaves as in Figure 3, shifting income into the pass-through (ordinary) base. Source: The Budget Lab Tax-Simulator, dials-v6 surrogate.
