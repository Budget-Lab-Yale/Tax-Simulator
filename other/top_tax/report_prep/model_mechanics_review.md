# Source material: what the Tax-Simulator top-of-distribution machinery does, what is novel, and what it misses

*Prepared from a read of the model code and design docs on branch `wealth`, repo root `/nfs/roberts/project/pi_nrs36/jar335/Repositories/Tax-Simulator`. Parameter values are as shipped in code as of 2026-07-16. Where a value is a placeholder or a residual rather than an independent measurement, it is flagged. (Generated by a review agent for the report-prep dossier; see report_prep/README note in the dossier.)*

## 1. Mechanism inventory

The model raises revenue estimates by running each tax reform through a stack of channels that adjust what taxpayers report, when they realize gains, and how wealth compounds and passes at death. Six of these are genuinely behavioral or dynamic; the rest are mechanical bookkeeping that lets one reform's effects show up in another base. The static estimate turns all of them off (it is the pure law-only counterfactual); the conventional estimate turns them on. None of these is a real-economy response — see §4.

### Capital-gains realization ("kg_dynamics")
*Code: `src/sim/kg_dynamics.R`; applier `config/scenarios/behavior/kg_dynamics/turnover.R`.*

This is the model's centerpiece and its main claim to novelty. It represents an investor's decision of how much of an unrealized capital gain to cash in each year, knowing that (a) selling triggers tax now, (b) holding defers it, and (c) dying with the gain may forgive it entirely (step-up basis), pass it to heirs (carryover), or trigger it at death (deemed realization). The model solves this trade-off as a dynamic optimization ("Bellman") for representative cells defined by age and asset type, then applies the resulting realization rates to individual records. When the gains rate goes up, the marginal cost of selling rises, so realizations fall — the classic "lock-in." The governing parameter is **η ≈ 2.4825** (`KG_DYN_DEFAULT_ETA`), which the code calls the long-run realization semi-elasticity; because the whole pool of gains responds, η is also the aggregate elasticity, and the revenue-maximizing gains rate is roughly 1/η ≈ 40%. η is calibrated so the full model reproduces a preferred permanent realization elasticity of −0.6 (inside the Dowd–McClelland–Muthitacharoen and Agersnap–Zidar empirical range). A separate short-run **timing overlay** (`KG_DYN_TIMEABLE_SHARE = 0.2542`) lets a fixed fraction of realizations jump forward or back one year around an announced change, netting to zero in the long run. The death regime enters through a "forgiveness value" `F = (1 − c_φ)·τ`, where **c_φ = 0 under step-up, θ under carryover, 1 under deemed realization** (`kg_dynamics.R:931`): step-up gives the strongest lock-in incentive, deemed realization eliminates it (F = 0). A deemed-realization valuation haircut of **0.25** (`KG_DYN_DEEMED_AVOIDANCE`) discounts the tax actually collected at forced death-realization, matching JCT-style assumptions.

### Income shifting / ETI ("σ conversion" + entity shifting + evasion)
*Code: `config/scenarios/behavior/conversion/sigma.R` + `src/sim/sigma_conversion.R`; `config/scenarios/behavior/entity_shifting/pearce_prisinzano.R`; `config/scenarios/behavior/evasion/debacker.R`. Plain-English memo (archived): `other/top_tax/archive/sigma_explainer.md`.*

The model splits the elasticity of taxable income (ETI) into three distinct real-world channels rather than one reduced-form number:

- **σ conversion** — owner-managers who face a high top ordinary rate repackage salary and active pass-through income as unrealized equity appreciation (sweat equity, founder stock, retained C-corp value), taxed later as gains or forgiven at death. The response is `σ × ΔW × pool`, where the wedge `W = (ordinary MTR on the leg) − τ_eq`, and **τ_eq** is the expected present-value tax per dollar entering the gain state, computed off the kg machinery (so it falls with age under step-up, rises under deemed). **σ = 0.16** (`SIGMA_CONV`, updated from 0.08 on 2026-07-12). σ is a *residual*: it is set so the full stack lands the top ETI at 0.25 (the Saez–Slemrod–Giertz central), given whatever entity-shifting and evasion contribute — so it must be re-derived whenever those change. Converted dollars are injected into the kg gain state, not added to `kg_lt` directly; conservation is asserted each year.
- **Entity shifting** (Pearce–Prisinzano) — business income migrates across the C-corp / pass-through boundary as the corporate-vs-individual tax differential moves. Semi-elasticity **e = 0.3788/0.6 ≈ 0.63**; **α = 0.45** of corporate earnings assumed distributed currently. The retained share used to be priced with a fixed "benefit-of-deferral" stub **β = 0.25**, but when kg is active it is now priced with the model's own τ_eq (so the C-corp retention shelter collapses endogenously under deemed-at-death).
- **Evasion** (DeBacker–Heim–Yuskavage) — a leak, not a relabeling: income leaves the tax system when the rate on a low-visibility source rises. Net-of-tax elasticities **0.046 (Schedule C/F), 0.052 (partnership/S-corp), 0.040 (rent)**; wages, interest, dividends do not respond (third-party reported). A top-end multiplier (`EVASION_TOPEND_MULT`, default 1.0, sweepable to 1.5–2.0) proxies under-detected sophisticated evasion.

Module order is pinned and enforced (`kg_dynamics → conversion/sigma → entity_shifting → evasion`) so the three never move the same dollar twice.

### Estate margins
*Calculator: `src/calc/functions/tax/estate.R`; weights/mortality: `src/sim/estate.R`; own-rate response: `config/scenarios/behavior/estate/avoidance.R`; frozen measurement: `config/estate/estate_valuation_params.yaml`; build report: `other/top_tax/estate_margins_build_report.html`.*

Estate tax is computed per record as an expected value conditional on death this year, with mortality applied only at aggregation. Law (exemption, rate schedule, portability, deductibility of income tax at death) is reform-overridable; the **measurement bridge** from economic wealth to reported gross estate is frozen and never overridable — **r = 0.951** (overall valuation/reporting factor), **ρ_pt = 0.612** (closely-held pass-through discount), **γ = 0.0914** (lifetime-gift add-back), and a donor-clone **cluster death-weight cap = 300**. The model deliberately never sets baseline estate *levels* (a known growth-slope disagreement with CBO); it contributes reform *deltas* only. Two behavioral margins were added 2026-07-12: (a) an **estate-exposure discount on the CG death value** — for an estate-taxable decedent, CG tax at death shrinks the taxable estate via the Sec. 2053 deduction, so the effective CG rate near death is τ·(1−e), and the kg Bellman now prices this (so estate reforms move realization behavior for the first time); (b) a **reported-estate own-rate response** (Kopczuk–Slemrod net-of-tax power form), elasticity **ε = 0.16** (band 0.10–0.22, `ESTATE_REPORT_EPS`), keyed strictly off the *change* in the estate rate (level avoidance is already in the frozen bridge). When the estate rate rises, reported gross estate shrinks by `1 − ((1−τ_S)/(1−τ_B))^ε`; heirs still inherit the hidden wealth in full (only the taxable base moves).

### Wealth-tax base and avoidance
*Calculator: `src/calc/functions/tax/wealth.R`; avoidance + hidden ledger: `config/scenarios/behavior/wealth/avoidance.R`; example law: `config/scenarios/tax_law/public/wealth_tax_warren/wealth.yaml`.*

A wealth tax is modeled as "the estate tax minus death" — a graduated schedule applied directly to economic net worth (raw assets minus debts, no valuation discount, no mortality). The Warren example is 0% below $50M, 2% to $1B, 3% above; a "nickel-and-dime" variant runs 5%/10%. Avoidance uses two semi-elasticities of reported taxable wealth with respect to the marginal wealth rate: **public_e = −7** (marketable/liquid) and **private_e = −17** (closely-held), author-accepted, seeded from the standalone Wealth-Tax-Simulator; at a 3% rate they imply roughly **19% / 40% erosion** of reported marketable / closely-held wealth. When the wealth rate goes up, reported net worth falls by `exp(mtr·e)` per class.

### Wealth-dynamics bathtub
*Code: `src/sim/wealth_dynamics.R` + `src/sim/cohort_bathtub.R`; calibration: `other/wealth_dynamics/default_s_calibration.md`; profiles: `config/wealth/profiles/default/s.csv`; params: `config/wealth/wealth_financing_params.yaml`.*

This is a mechanical, conventional-side *financing* channel — explicitly **not** a savings elasticity. A share **s = 1 − MPC** of the net above-baseline during-life tax increase (income + payroll − deemed + wealth, minus any exogenous income shock) is paid out of wealth rather than consumption; that drained wealth compounds forward and drains into the estate and capital-income bases at death. The saving share s varies by age and within-age net-worth percentile, from **≈ 0.04–0.10 at the bottom to ≈ 0.80 at the top** (calibrated to a persistent-flow anchor: Straub's ε≈0.7 consumption elasticity, Mian–Straub–Sufi top-1% saving shares, Dynan–Skinner–Zeldes), with an age tilt (peak in the 45–64 accumulation years, attenuated to zero at the very top per De Nardi–French–Jones). The **dollar-weighted effective s for top-concentrated reforms is ≈ 0.78**. The wealth gap grows each year by a kernel `G = (1 + r_total) − s·(τ·y + τ_w)` where `r_total` is nominal GDP-per-capita growth and the second term is the tax-foregone drag. When a during-life tax goes up, the household's wealth trajectory bends down, and the estate/wealth/capital-income bases it would have fed shrink over the horizon. The channel is ON model-wide by default; opt out with `wealth_financing = none`. Measured self-erosion: a Warren wealth tax loses ≈ 6.2% of its conventional score to this drain.

### Corporate incidence
*Code: `src/sim/corp_incidence.R`; design: `other/corporate_incidence/CONSIDERATIONS.md`, `other/corporate_incidence/FORMAL_MODEL.md`.*

A mechanical, revenue-side channel that maps the gross corporate receipts delta (from an off-model estimate) onto individual records rather than smearing it. A corporate rate hike (i) cuts shareholder flows — dividends, interest, rent, pass-through income; (ii) marks down exposed equity stocks on the balance sheet (**equities exposure 1.0, DC retirement 0.55, trusts 0.50, real-estate funds 0.30**; basis never scales; DB pensions never debited); (iii) adjusts capital-gain bases; (iv) drains the bathtub; and (v) produces an **endogenous individual-tax offset that simply materializes in conventional receipts**. The normal-return share of the wedge (shifted vs capitalized) is **σ_N = 0.375** (`CORP_SIGMA_N`); the C-corp share of the normal-capital stock is **κ = 0.40**; C-corp shares of dividends and gains are **0.85** and **0.50**. Capitalization is computed as the perfect-foresight present value of the legislated rate path (no free capitalization coefficient). Activation is fail-closed: it turns on only when the corporate input carries explicit metadata declaring it a gross-of-offset rate provision. **All `CORP_*` constants are disclosed Phase-0c placeholders.**

### Hidden ledger (concealment consistency)
*Design: `other/top_tax/hidden_ledger_design.md`; implemented across `wealth/avoidance.R`, `estate/avoidance.R`, `evasion/debacker.R`, `calc_estate`.*

A single organizing rule: money that leaves the tax authority's sight in one base must leave the others too. Wealth-tax avoidance is split into **concealment** (the money disappears — its income flows and estate value vanish from reported bases) and **legal valuation gaming** (assessed value lowballed, income still visible). The concealment shares are **CHI_PUB = 1.0** (you cannot lowball an exchange price, so avoiding marketable wealth means hiding it) and **CHI_PRIV = 0.5** (private-business valuation discounts are half real). Concealed marketable assets stop producing reported interest/dividends/gains; concealed closely-held assets stop producing reported pass-through income; both escape the reported estate. A reverse link makes evaded business income pull the matching assets out of the reported wealth and estate bases. A conservation identity (`reported + hidden = pre-avoidance`) is hard-asserted every year. Measured on a wealth-tax scenario: concealment pulls **income tax down ≈ $34–35B/yr and estate revenue down ≈ $7–8B/yr** relative to a no-concealment run, on ≈ $2.0T marketable / $2.4T closely-held concealed wealth.

## 2. Cross-base interaction map

The model's reason for existence is that raising rates on the same small group through several statutes at once double-counts, because the bases overlap. Below, every pairwise interaction it captures, tagged **[M]** mechanical (bookkeeping flows one base's change into another), **[B]** behavioral (a taxpayer decision responds), or **[✗]** not captured.

**Captured behaviorally:**

1. **Ordinary rate → capital-gains base** [B]. A higher top ordinary rate widens the salary-vs-equity wedge; σ conversion moves labor compensation into the unrealized-gain state, shrinking the ordinary and payroll bases now and feeding the gains base later. (`sigma.R`)
2. **Ordinary/QBI rate ↔ corporate base** [B]. The C-vs-pass-through differential drives income across the entity boundary; QBI repeal raises the effective pass-through rate and moves the differential. (`pearce_prisinzano.R`)
3. **Capital-gains rate ↔ estate tax (at death)** [B]. For estate-taxable decedents the CG tax at death is offset by the Sec. 2053 deduction, so the effective death rate is τ·(1−e); the kg Bellman prices this, so estate reforms move realization and CG reforms are worth less near death for exposed cells. (`kg_dynamics.R:931`, estate-margins build)
4. **Deemed realization at death ↔ realization behavior** [B]. Step-up (F = τ) creates strong lock-in; deemed (F = 0) eliminates the lock-in payoff, so realizations barely respond to a rate hike and deferred gains are recaptured at death. The crown interaction.
5. **Wealth tax ↔ capital-gains realization** [B]. Holding an unrealized gain defers CG tax, and that deferred liability sits in the wealth base costing τ_w·τ per year; the Bellman's carrying-cost term `h` unlocks realizations under a wealth tax. A 1% wealth tax erodes roughly half the deferral advantage. (wealth-carry build)
6. **Income rate ↔ evasion leak** [B]; **wealth/estate rate ↔ concealment** [B], with the cross-base consistency links below.

**Captured mechanically:**

7. **Corporate rate → capital-gains, estate, and wealth bases** [M]. A corporate hike marks down exposed equity on the balance sheet → smaller net worth → smaller wealth-tax base, smaller gross estate, smaller unrealized-gain state → less wealth/estate/deemed revenue, over decades. (`corp_incidence.R`)
8. **Corporate rate → individual income tax** [M, endogenous]. Cut flows and adjusted gains feed the calculator, so individual receipts fall automatically — the offset JCT/OTA bury inside the corporate line, made explicit.
9. **During-life taxes (income, payroll, CG, wealth) → estate + future capital-income bases** [M]. The bathtub drains a share of any during-life tax increase out of wealth, shrinking what compounds into the estate and capital-income bases. This is the channel that makes a gains-rate hike partly *resurface* later as estate/wealth revenue.
10. **Deemed realization → estate base** [M]. Deemed CG tax at death is income-tax-at-death deductible, shrinking the taxable estate (`estate_income_tax_ded` in `calc_estate`).
11. **Wealth-tax concealment → income + estate bases** [M/B]. Concealed assets stop producing reported dividends/interest/pass-through income and escape the reported estate. (hidden ledger)
12. **Income evasion → wealth + estate bases** [M/B]. Evaded business income pulls the matching closely-held assets out of the reported wealth and estate bases (R3 link).
13. **Payroll wage cap / SECA ↔ pass-through income** [M]. Every reporting module co-scales the SECA earner-split legs, so the payroll base moves consistently with any pass-through income response.
14. **NIIT ↔ everything** [M]. NIIT rides inside the composite marginal tax rates the model measures, so it enters the Bellman and the σ/entity wedges automatically.

**Not captured:**

15. **Corporate rate → realization behavior or σ conversion price** [✗]. The kg Bellman and τ_eq see only `mtr_kg_lt` — no corporate layer — so a corporate rate change does not move realization or the conversion price (only the mechanical markdown flows through). (`margins_gap_assessment.md` §2.3)
16. **Wealth-tax / estate-tax terms in the σ conversion price** [✗]. σ prices only ordinary-vs-τ_eq; the founder-equity path is treated as corporate-tax-free and wealth/estate-free.
17. **Capital-gains rate ↔ appreciated-asset charitable giving** [✗]. Charity responds only to `char_cash` at the itemized-deduction price; the well-documented "donate stock instead of selling" margin is not modeled.
18. **Dividend/CG differential → payout behavior** [✗] (Chetty–Saez; α fixed at 0.45). **Estate rate → inter-vivos gift timing** [✗] (γ is measurement, not response). **Ordinary rate → muni/portfolio clientele shift** [✗].

## 3. Novel quantifiable claims

Claims this model supports that JCT/CBO/TPC conventions do not (or bury). Each cites the mechanism and the output that backs it.

1. **Revenue (Laffer) curves that differ by death-basis regime.** *Claim:* the revenue-maximizing capital-gains rate and the revenue level both depend on whether gains get step-up, carryover, or deemed realization at death — deemed roughly doubles the revenue of a gains-rate hike relative to step-up and pushes the peak rate higher. *Novel because* standard scorekeepers apply one realization elasticity regardless of death treatment. *Output:* `other/kg_model_tests/revmax_eta_v1_result.md` and `config/runscripts/tests/revmax_cg.csv` (levels sharply ordered step-up < carryover < deemed, deemed ≈ 2× step-up; note these specific numbers are spec-v2 and superseded — the qualitative ordering and the "peak near 1/η ≈ 40%" survive to the current single-pool spec).
2. **The sign of "leakage" flips under deemed realization.** *Claim:* under step-up, raising the gains rate loses revenue to lock-in (conventional < static, positive leakage); under deemed realization the lock-in escape is gone and deferred gains are recaptured at death, so the conventional estimate can exceed the static one. *Novel because* JCT scores the realization response as a pure loss irrespective of the death regime. *Output:* the destination-ledger (Object A) receipts-by-head, and the estate-margins ordering (baseline $37.9B < conventional $62.2B < static $75.1B under a 2009-parameters estate reform, 2027).
3. **Package-vs-parts overstatement.** *Claim:* summing standalone scores overstates a combined top-of-distribution package because the bases overlap; the full-stack combo comes in roughly 9% below the sum of its parts. *Novel because* no scorekeeper runs a joint factorial on one record-level balance sheet to net out base overlap. *Output:* the 128-combo factorial and the parts-vs-whole exhibit (`other/top_tax/VISION.md` §2.7).
4. **Realized-revenue ETR vs welfare-burden ETR, on an accrual denominator.** *Claim:* the effective rate the law *asks* of the top 0.01% and the rate the government actually *collects* diverge sharply — roughly 49% asked vs 37% collected — and the gap is, to first order, the deadweight loss of avoidance. *Novel because* TPC uses a cash-income denominator and does not separate welfare burden from realized revenue. *Output:* `static/supplemental/distribution_etrs.csv`; dials build (top 0.01%: 23.7% baseline → 48.9% ask → 37.3% collected).
5. **Wealth-tax-to-capital-gains interaction through the deferred-liability carrying cost.** *Claim:* a 1% wealth tax measurably unlocks capital-gains realizations (+3.6% on the wealth dial, +$127B conventional over ten years) because it erases about half the value of deferral. *Novel because* no standard model links a wealth tax to gains-realization timing. *Output:* wealth-carry build T1–T5; `carry_h` in the kg state.
6. **A corporate rate hike's decade-scale drag on estate and wealth-tax revenue.** *Claim:* marking down equity on household balance sheets shrinks the estate and wealth-tax bases for decades. *Novel because,* in the design doc's words, "nobody scores how much a corporate rate hike reduces estate-tax and wealth-tax revenue over 30 years." *Output:* `corp_incidence` conservation diagnostics and receipts-by-head.
7. **The embedded corporate individual-tax offset, made explicit.** *Claim:* JCT/OTA already net an individual income-tax offset into the corporate revenue line (Nunns 2012); this model materializes it as an explicit, micro-founded receipts delta on the right households. *Novel because* it un-hides a number that is invisible in provision-level scores. *Output:* corporate endogenous-offset receipts.
8. **The estate tax was already offsetting CG lock-in at the top.** *Claim:* for the top decile of the gain stock, ≈ 18 cents of every marginal dollar of death-forgiven CG tax was already clawed back through the estate tax under current law (66% of gain dollars have zero estate exposure — it is entirely a top-decile phenomenon), so the standard lock-in story overstated the high-net-worth investor's gain from holding. *Novel because* no scorekeeper prices the CG-death-value × estate-tax interaction cell by cell. *Output:* `kg_estate_exposure_diag_{t}.csv`.
9. **Cross-base concealment consistency.** *Claim:* a dollar hidden from a wealth tax also disappears from reported dividends and from the taxable estate, so a wealth tax mechanically erodes income and estate revenue too (≈ −$34–35B/yr income, ≈ −$7–8B/yr estate). *Novel because* nobody enforces that avoidance in one base shrinks the others. *Output:* `conventional/supplemental/hidden_ledger_{t}.csv`.

## 4. Caveats and omissions register

An honest report should carry all of these.

**Real economic responses — entirely absent.** Every channel is a *reporting, timing, avoidance, or financing* response. There is **no labor-supply response, no real saving/rate-of-return elasticity, and no real investment response.** The wealth bathtub is explicitly a *financing* channel (what share of a tax is paid out of wealth vs consumption), not a behavioral savings elasticity. This means the model's behavioral cost is best read as a *floor* on the true efficiency cost of these taxes — it captures the paper-shuffling response, not the "work/save/invest less" response.

**Migration and expatriation — not separately modeled.** They are subsumed into the reduced-form wealth-avoidance elasticity (−7/−17), and the wealth-tax estimate is disclosed as a **ceiling** on that basis. The model cannot decompose erosion into relocation vs domestic restructuring.

**No dynamic general-equilibrium / macro feedback.** GNP is held fixed (JCT convention). The corporate channel deliberately omits the long-run labor-incidence effect (smaller capital stock → lower wages), which is a GE dynamic; it models flows and the stock markdown only.

**Specific avoidance vehicles not modeled.** Opportunity-Zone funds, "buy-borrow-die" borrowing against appreciated assets, GRATs/trust structures as such, charitable-remainder strategies, and the appreciated-asset charitable-giving margin are **not** modeled. Dividend payout responses to a dividend/CG differential, inter-vivos gift-timing responses to estate-rate changes, and portfolio clientele shifts to municipals are **not** modeled (`margins_gap_assessment.md` §2.1, §3).

**Structural blind spots in the realization engine.** The kg Bellman prices off a single tax rate (`mtr_kg_lt`): the corporate rate does not move realization or the σ conversion price at all, and wealth/estate terms do not enter the σ conversion price. (Wealth and estate terms *were* added to the Bellman itself in July 2026 — the wealth carrying cost and the estate death-value discount — but the σ conversion price and the corporate-in-Bellman gaps remain open.)

**Uncertain or placeholder calibrations — the honest range-setters:**
- **All `CORP_*` constants are Phase-0c placeholders** (σ_N = 0.375, κ = 0.40, ω_div = 0.85, ω_kg = 0.50, DC/trust/RE-fund equity exposures 0.55/0.50/0.30, θ = 1.0, θ_res = 0.40) — disclosed as gate 4. The corporate card sits on these.
- **σ = 0.16 is a residual, not an independent measurement** — set to hit a top ETI of 0.25 given the rest of the stack; it re-derives whenever entity-shifting, evasion, or charity change (it already moved 0.08 → 0.16 when two other commits landed).
- **Wealth-avoidance −7/−17** are author-accepted, seeded from a sister model; a sensitivity band is recommended, not shipped.
- **Estate own-rate ε = 0.16** bundles timing, avoidance, valuation, *and* some real accumulation and charitable planning; band 0.10–0.22. The elasticity is applied to reported *gross* while Kopczuk–Slemrod is of the reported *taxable* estate — a slight overstatement worst near a lowered exemption (measured 12.9% giveback under a 2009-parameters reform; disclosed).
- **η = 2.4825 carries a measured +3.7% drift.** The estate-death-value discount weakened the calibrated realization elasticity; re-pinning to ≈ 2.49 is recommended but not yet done, and shipped atlas/dials vintages carry the pre-drift η.
- **Deemed-avoidance haircut 0.25** and the applier allocation 0.5 are data-calibration placeholders.
- **s-profile simplifications:** symmetric for hikes and cuts, a single static surface (year-1 drain modestly overstated at the top), and income-share anchors mapped onto net-worth ranks (soft band edges).
- **CHI_PUB = 1.0 / CHI_PRIV = 0.5** are reduced-form rulings.

**Measurement issues.**
- Tax-Data is a **repeated cross-section, not a panel**, so only stock events (realization, death) are consistent across years — there is no true annual mark-to-market flow.
- A **wages residual** (`wages1 + wages2 ≠ wages`) produces a nonzero baseline-on-baseline delta that must be netted before interpreting reform deltas.
- **Donor-clone clusters** in Tax-Data required the estate cluster death-weight cap (300); `age1` is topcoded at 80.
- The **estate valuation bridge is frozen and vintage-pinned** (r = 0.951, ρ_pt = 0.612); the model never sets baseline estate *levels* — only deltas — a known growth-slope disagreement with CBO.

**Known bug in shipped vintages.** The estate-avoidance activation bug (estate-only runscript rows previously loaded no reported-estate response, and evasion never propagated into estates) was fixed 2026-07-16, but shipped dials/atlas vintages predating the fix still carry it.

## 5. Writer's ammunition

1. Summing the standalone scores double-counts the base: the same dollar is capital income during life, an estate at death, and a wealth-tax base in between. Score them together and roughly a tenth of the naive total turns out to be overlap.
2. Repealing step-up before raising the gains rate is fixing the leak before raising the water level — the rate hike collects far more once death no longer forgives the gain.
3. Under step-up, a higher gains rate mostly buys you lock-in. Under deemed realization the same rate hike raises about twice the revenue, because the gain can no longer wait out the taxpayer's death.
4. Lock-in is not a law of nature; it is an option to die with the gain. Take the option away and the behavioral response to a rate hike shrinks.
5. A wealth tax is a slow solvent for capital-gains lock-in: every year you defer a dollar of gain, the deferred tax you are sitting on is itself taxed. A 1% wealth tax erases about half the advantage of waiting.
6. For the top of the gain distribution, the estate tax was already clawing back about 18 cents of every dollar of gains tax that death forgives — so the textbook lock-in story overstated how much high-net-worth investors gain by holding.
7. A corporate rate hike is a stealth tax on estates and wealth: it marks down the equity sitting in net worth, so the estate and wealth-tax bases shrink for decades. No conventional score captures that.
8. The individual-tax offset to a corporate rate change is not new — JCT and OTA have always buried it inside the corporate line. This model just moves it into daylight and puts it on the right households.
9. The model draws a hard wall between hiding money and revaluing it: concealed wealth vanishes from the income and estate bases too, while a mere valuation discount does not.
10. Evasion is a leak; conversion is a detour. A dollar the owner-manager repackages as equity comes back later as a capital gain; a dollar hidden from an auditor never comes back at all.
11. Two effective tax rates tell two different stories: the rate the law asks of the top 0.01% and the rate the government actually collects after avoidance. On these numbers the ask is near 49% and the take is near 37%.
12. The gap between the welfare-burden ETR and the realized-revenue ETR is, to first order, the deadweight loss of avoidance — the resources burned rerouting income rather than the revenue raised.
13. Behavior does not so much destroy revenue as relocate it. A gains-rate hike that leaks from the gains base partly resurfaces later as estate and wealth-tax revenue, when the deferred gains finally die.
14. The wealth bathtub is a financing story, not a savings elasticity: it asks what share of a tax increase the rich pay out of wealth rather than consumption, and follows that drained wealth into the estate. For top-concentrated reforms that share is about three-quarters.
15. Every avoidance number here is a reporting response, not a real one. Nobody in this model works less, saves less, or invests less — they relabel, defer, conceal, and restructure. That makes these estimates a floor on behavioral cost, not a ceiling.
16. The wealth-tax estimate is a ceiling: migration and expatriation are folded into a single avoidance elasticity, so the model cannot tell you how much of the erosion is a move to Geneva versus a Cayman LLC.
17. A wealth tax partly finances itself away twice over — assets get hidden, so income and estate revenue fall too, and the tax that is paid drains the wealth that would have been taxed next year.
18. Pushing the top gains rate to 43.4% is not obviously past the peak: with the whole gain pool responding, the revenue-maximizing rate sits near 40%, and closing the step-up escape pushes that peak higher still.
