Step-up caps the capital-gains rate as a revenue tool; reforming it raises the ceiling

Conventional revenue as % of GDP, third decade (FY2047–56) · behavioral responses included · shared scale

Deemed realization at death Carryover basis Current law — step-up in basis

Under step-up, the capital-gains rate stops paying around 43.8%. Revenue peaks near a 43.8% rate at about 0.13% of GDP and then declines — extra rate loses money, because it pushes more gains to be held until death and escape tax entirely under step-up. (The turnover is behavioral: the old mechanical ceiling at the ordinary top rate has been removed, so the rate is free to bite higher until deferral bends the curve back down.) Reforming step-up changes the picture two ways. It raises the starting level: at today's 23.8% rate, taxing gains at death already collects ~0.17% of GDP (carryover ~0.08%) with no rate change at all. And it keeps the rate productive: with deferral closed, the same rate increases keep pulling in revenue, reaching ~0.59% of GDP at 53.8% — roughly 4× the step-up curve at its own peak. Carryover sits between the two, peaking near 48.8%. Rates on the axis are statutory capital-gains rates plus the 3.8% net investment income tax, which the model holds at current law throughout. Source: Tax-Simulator, revmax grid on the dials-v6 physics (net-of-tax realization form, uncapped capital-gains rate); values are total conventional revenue over the third projection decade (FY2047–56) as a share of cumulative GDP over that decade. All three curves are direct model runs at every plotted point — no surrogate composition. Curves are total revenue, so they include the death regime's own yield; the shape, not any single number, is the point.
