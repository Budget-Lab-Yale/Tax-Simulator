Effective federal tax rates under current law: cash vs. accrual income, 2027

All federal taxes (individual income, payroll, expected estate tax at death, and allocated corporate tax) as a share of pre-tax income, Budget Lab Tax-Simulator static baseline. Income groups are ranked by cash (expanded) income; the rightmost group is the model’s 400 wealthiest tax units, ranked by net worth.

Cash income (realized) Accrual income (Haig–Simons)

Concepts. Cash = expanded income as realized (wages, dividends, interest, realized capital gains, transfers, etc.). Accrual = Haig–Simons: accrued capital gains replace realizations in the denominator. Both denominators include each group’s allocated share of corporate tax; numerators are identical dollars, so the two lines differ only in the income concept.

Taxes included. Individual income tax (net of refundable credits — the bottom quintile’s income-tax component is negative), payroll tax, expected estate tax at death, and baseline corporate receipts allocated in proportion to capital income. Current law has no wealth tax or VAT. State and local taxes are excluded.

Top 400. The 400 highest-net-worth tax units in the model (minimum net worth $4.1 billion; combined $7.7 trillion), the model’s Forbes-400 analog. Their estate-tax component rounds to zero because the estate module caps the death weights of these cloned records (the cluster guard), so expected estate tax does not register in a single-year snapshot. Ranking this group by wealth while the rest of the chart ranks by income follows the convention of Saez–Zucman-style presentations.

Comparison. The White House CEA (2021) estimated the top 400 paid 8.2% of income including unrealized gains in federal individual income tax (2010–2018 average). The comparable component here is 6.5%; the 8.1% shown adds payroll and allocated corporate tax.
