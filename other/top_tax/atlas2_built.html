Revenue from higher taxes at the top

There are many ways to raise taxes on high-income households, and each has its own revenue estimate when scored on its own. But these policies tax overlapping bases and change how people respond to each other's rates, so combining them does not raise the sum of their separate estimates.

This page lets you combine eight top-tax policies at rates and thresholds you set, using the Budget Lab's tax microsimulation model. Revenue is cumulative over the decade selected below, shown in dollars and as a share of that decade's GDP.

How policies stack

Static estimate and collected revenue, one policy at a time

Each row adds one policy on top of the rows above it. The slim bar is the static estimate; the full bar is what the package collects once behavior responds. Both are split by the tax base where the revenue is collected. The final row is the whole package. Its total does not change when you reorder the rows: reordering changes only how the shared interaction is attributed across policies, not the total. Drag a row by its handle to change the order.

The revenue frontier

Each point is one package from the lattice of settings. Its horizontal position is the revenue the package collects over the selected decade, as a share of that decade's GDP. Toggling the axis replaces this with the increase in the top 0.1 percent's realized effective tax rate on accrual income in 2027. Its vertical position is the difference between collected and static revenue, in cents per dollar collected: positive when behavior raises revenue, negative when it reduces it. A package is dominated when another package collects more with a smaller behavioral loss; the packages that nothing dominates form the frontier. Points above the zero line collect more than their static estimate, which happens when taxing gains at death removes the incentive to defer realizations. Your current selection is marked. The effective-tax-rate axis is measured in the impact year (2027) and does not change with the decade toggle; the vertical axis does.

Horizontal axis

Behavioral responses included

  • Capital-gains realization: timing and permanent responses, including how taxing gains at death changes the incentive to defer
  • Conversion of ordinary income into capital gains
  • Entity shifting between pass-through and C-corporation form, including its payroll-tax base effect
  • Underreporting and evasion
  • Charitable giving
  • Wealth-tax avoidance, applied consistently to the wealth, income, and estate bases

Mechanical channels included

  • Part of new taxes is financed out of saving, which compounds over time into smaller estates and less capital income
  • Corporate incidence on households: lower dividends, interest, rent, and pass-through income, and marked-down equity values that reduce future realized gains
  • Interactions inside the tax calculation itself: rate stacking, base broadening, and revenue moving across bases (income, payroll, corporate, estate, wealth)

Not included: labor-supply responses, macroeconomic feedback, and changes to tax enforcement.

Model output

Model output, interpolated. Figures come from the top-tax dials batch of the Budget Lab Tax-Simulator: each policy run at a set of anchor settings, every pair run together at reference settings, and the four policies that share the capital-gains-at-death channel also run three at a time. Any dial setting in between is interpolated with a solo + pairwise + triple decomposition, and the interpolation is validated against held-out model runs of full multi-policy packages at off-anchor settings; the badge states the verified bound. The corporate dial is interpolated between anchor runs at 24.5%, 28%, 31.5% and 35%, like the other rate dials; the rate is scored inside the model against the CBO corporate receipts baseline. Interpolation is most stretched where an annual wealth tax and the estate tax are both pushed to their extreme settings at once: that combination carries a non-linear interaction the decomposition captures only approximately, so those far-corner packages can run up to roughly 2.6% off the corresponding full model run (vs. under 1% across the rest of the space); every setting shown remains a first-decade estimate within the badge's stated bound.

Revenue figures are 10-year cumulative dollars over the decade selected at the top of the page (first, second, or third decade after enactment), with the share of GDP (10-yr revenue ÷ that decade's cumulative fiscal-year nominal GDP) shown alongside. The distribution card always shows 2027, the impact year, regardless of the selected decade. Policies, effective 2027: top ordinary rate · top capital-gains & dividends rate · corporate rate · annual wealth tax (rate and threshold) · treatment of gains at death (step-up, carryover, or taxed at death) · estate tax (top rate and exemption) · repeal of the QBI deduction · elimination of the Social Security taxable maximum.
