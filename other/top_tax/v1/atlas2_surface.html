One surface per death regime: the ten-year revenue attributable to the capital-gains rate change — its stacked-last score, on top of the ordinary-rate change and the death regime — at every combination of the two top rates. Where a sheet crests along the capital-gains axis, that rate is revenue-maximizing under that regime; taxing gains at death lifts the whole sheet and pushes the crest to higher rates. Revenue-vs-rate is evaluated at the capital-gains anchors (every 5 points) with the same measured solo + pairwise + triple decomposition the dials explorer uses, on the same run data — the deemed and carryover surfaces use interaction terms measured at each ladder position, never scaled from another position. Each sheet is the capital-gains policy's stacked-last score: the package total minus the same package without the capital-gains change, so interactions with the ordinary rate and the death regime accrue to the capital-gains line — the same last-in-the-stack convention as a conventional stacked estimate. The score is piecewise-linear between the 5-point rate anchors; where a sheet crests, that regime's capital-gains rate is revenue-maximizing. The flattened views drop one axis: the solid line holds the removed dimension at a reference (ordinary rate at current law, or cg at 40%) and the shaded band spans its range.
