What was built

design in brief

A wealth tax is structurally the estate tax minus death — a balance-sheet/stock tax that lives in the do_taxes() chain, aggregates with weights, books receipts, and feeds distribution, but stripped of mortality, DSUE/portability, the death-time valuation adjustments, and the estate's FY t+1 receipts lag.

One design choice ties it together: a materialized net_worth column (economic Σ assets − Σ debts, raw dollars) computed once per record in run_one_year(). That single column plays three roles — it is the calc_wealth() base, the +$1 bump target that lets the standard next-dollar MTR machinery price the marginal statutory wealth rate, and the isolation point the conventional-pass avoidance module overwrites. Because avoidance touches only net_worth and never the raw value.* columns, estate liability and capital income are structurally unaffected. liab_wealth stays a separate column, never folded into income tax.

All revenue/totals/distribution wiring lives in functions the SLURM pipeline already shares (write_pass_outputs, calc_receipts, calc_rev_est, process_for_distribution), so no aggregate.R mirror edits were needed — the new source files are auto-sourced by reconstitute_environment().

Cross-model comparison — apples-to-apples

filing-blind nickel-dime · identical law + elasticities · $B, CY

The first comparison looked off — TS scored 0.79× the standalone and ~2.3× fewer taxable units. Tracing it: the gap was not the data, it was one law difference. TS doubles the exemption for couples (MFJ → $100M), so married units between $50M–$100M escape; WTS is filing-blind ($50M for everyone). The arithmetic closes exactly — nonmarried ≥$50M (51,136) + married ≥$100M (79,373) = 130,509, the TS taxable count to the unit. Making TS filing-blind too (a flat filing_status_mapper) puts the two models on identical law and identical avoidance elasticities; the residual is then pure data.

Caveats & deferred work

read the levels as illustrative, not published estimates

Donor-clone top tail is uncapped for a living tax

Tax-Data replicates thin top-wealth pools into byte-identical donor clones. The estate model caps their death weight (~300/cluster), but an annual tax weights every living clone every year — inflating the very top of the base where a wealth tax concentrates (max single net worth here: $758B). A living-weight cap is deferred (plan P2); until then the static levels likely overstate the top tail.

Avoidance elasticities are seeded, not calibrated

public_e = −7, private_e = −17 are copied verbatim from the standalone so the behavioral comparison is apples-to-apples. They are deliberately extreme (near-total base disappearance at high rates) and must be re-justified on Tax-Data before any published conventional score. Flagged in the module's WEALTH_AVOID_PROVENANCE.

Fixed wealth stock (s = 0)

This build is the simple case: the stock is held fixed across years and the only behavior is within-year reported-base avoidance. The saving-financed decumulation "bathtub" (tax paid out of saving → smaller future estate & capital-income base) is a separate mechanism, deferred to purrfect-weaving-toucan.

Receipts lag to the filing-season FY (t+1)

A wealth tax is non-withheld, paid at filing the spring after the assessment year (CY t net worth → return Jan–Apr CY t+1 → FY t+1), so revenues_wealth_tax books a full year late, mechanically like the estate tax. The runscript therefore runs one year past the window (to 2031) so the last policy year's receipt isn't dropped. Note an open inconsistency: the model books ordinary non-withheld income/payroll tax in-year (a documented remit_taxes simplification), so the wealth tax is now timed like estate, not like the model's other non-withheld receipts.
